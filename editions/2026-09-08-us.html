Why join

The Census Bureau publishes Income, Poverty and Health Insurance Coverage in the United States: 2025 at 10am ET on September 15. Four hours later, we go live. Our guests give a few minutes each on what the new numbers say. Then we turn to what state reforms would do to child poverty: David walks through the Child Poverty Impact Dashboard live, building a reform, tracing one family’s taxes and benefits across the earnings range, and comparing statewide and district impacts.

Preview. The dashboard opens to the public on Tuesday, September 15.

Configure a reform, a state Child Tax Credit or EITC, a dependent exemption, a child allowance, a SNAP expansion, or a federal proposal such as the American Family Act, and see its impact on child poverty, state and federal budgets, income distribution, congressional districts, and individual households, for all 50 states and DC. Every estimate comes from PolicyEngine’s open-source microsimulation model over calibrated state and local-area microdata, and every result has a shareable link.

The dashboard goes public the morning of the 15th. Researchers, advocates, and legislative staff will leave knowing how to score a reform in their own state.

Also from PolicyEngine

PolicyBench

32 models on the board, and a page for running it on yours

PolicyBench tests how accurately language models calculate household taxes and benefits: 100 real households, 18 outputs, one identical request to every model. The August 22 board scores 32 models. GPT-5.6 Sol leads with 88.7% of amounts exact. Since July the board added Kimi K3 (second), Ox Alpha preview (fourth), Inkling (fifth), Grok 4.6 (eighth), and Gemini 3.7 Flash.

The leaderboard now carries a serving-sensitivity note. Claude models skip extended thinking when the answer tool call is forced, as it is in the board’s identical request. Re-run with tool choice set to auto, Claude Fable 5 scores 86.9%, which would rank second. Those runs sit beside the board as a labeled sensitivity, and the next board version moves every model to auto.

New: policybench.org/expand, for anyone who wants PolicyBench run on their region, population, or program.

New on policyengine.org

The PolicyEngine Scorecard puts our estimates next to external scores and analyses, starting with Urban Institute’s State of the Safety Net: nine programs across the US, 50 states, and DC, every external number beside its PolicyEngine counterpart with the difference shown. The misses stay on the page. Since launch it has added tariff-rate comparisons against the Yale Budget Lab and the Tax Policy Center.

In the model

Child care subsidy rules are now modeled for all 50 states and DC. New York, Illinois, and Texas landed in August, and each state’s program flows into household and population results alongside the rest. Nine state premium assistance programs joined the model in August too: Connecticut, Vermont, New Jersey, Massachusetts, Washington, New Mexico, Colorado, California, and Maryland.

The model also carries the first OBBBA implementation rules: the SNAP work requirement now covers adults aged 60 to 64, with the 30-hour work exemption and county-level waivers, and Medicaid community engagement rules include the hardship exceptions for hospitalization and long-distance care. That is 127 policyengine-us releases since our July newsletter.
